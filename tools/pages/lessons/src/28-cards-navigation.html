A stack of cards

A stack shows one card at a time. Each card is a screen of its own: a page of a book, a step of a quiz, a record in an address list. create card adds a card after the current one and goes to it; go moves between them.

go next card on the last card goes round to the first, and go previous card on the first to the last, so a stack is a ring of cards. You can also go first card, go to card 2, or go back to the card you were on before.

Which card is this?

the number of this card is its place in the stack, and the cardNames lists every card's name, a line each.

Each card gets ready: openCard

Going to a card sends it openCard, and leaving it sends closeCard. A handler in the stack's script hears them for every card; one in a card's script, only for that card.

Controls on every card: backgrounds

Many stacks want the same controls on every card: a title, Next and Back buttons, a field for each part of a record. Put them in a group and set the group's backgroundBehavior to true: every card you create after it gets the same group. It is one group, placed on many cards.

The field is shared, but each card keeps its own text in it: that is what a field's sharedText property, false unless you change it, means. So a stack of cards with a background is a little database, one record a card, the way HyperCard's address books worked. (The picture shows the stage as the example left it, on card 1; go to card 2 in the playground to see the other.)

In HyperCard a card's background was a layer of its own. OXT-Beyond, like LiveCode, makes a HyperCard background into a group with backgroundBehavior true when it imports a stack, and the scripts that said background field "Name" still work.

Deleting cards

delete card removes a card and the controls that are only on it. A stack always keeps at least one card.
