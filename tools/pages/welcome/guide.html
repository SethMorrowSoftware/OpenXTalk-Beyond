Coming from LiveCode

The IDE you know, carried on.

OXT-Beyond is LiveCode Community’s code, carried on in the open: the Tools palette, the Property Inspector, the Script Editor and the Dictionary you know, the same language, and your LiveCode Community 9 stacks as they are. Here is what opens, what differs, where your libraries went, and where every feature stands.

Every fact here names its release, as of 21 November 2026. Where something hasn’t been tested, it says so.

Start here

Try it in five minutes

Nothing changes in the LiveCode you have, and you can stop at any step.

  1. Check it

    Drop the stack on the stack checker. It reads the file in your browser, uploads nothing, and says what will work.

  2. Play it

    Open it in the Stack Player, in a browser tab, with nothing to install.

  3. Install beside LiveCode

    Download OXT-Beyond. It keeps its preferences in its own folders, so your LiveCode stays as it is.

  4. Open a copy

    OXT-Beyond saves in the 8.1 format, which LiveCode before 8.1 can’t open. Work on a copy, and keep the original.

  5. Tell us

    Something differs? Report it on GitHub with the checker’s report, or ask on the OpenXTalk forum.

Side by side

Install it next to LiveCode

OXT-Beyond keeps its preferences, caches and logs in its own folders, and LiveCode keeps its preferences elsewhere, so the two live side by side. The first start uses default preferences: nothing is copied over.

What’s different

Small things worth knowing

None of these changes what an old script sees. They are the places a LiveCode user may look twice.

Saving

OXT-Beyond saves in the 8.1 format. For an older LiveCode, save stack "Name" with format 7.0 writes an older one and drops what it can’t hold, as the Dictionary’s save entry warns.

New syntax stays here

Literal arrays compile in OXT-Beyond and LiveCode 10, not in LiveCode 9. A stack that has to go back should keep to the older syntax.

Android stacks keep their engine

A stack already set up for Android is built with LiveCode 9.6.3’s engine, as before, unless you tick the box for OXT-Beyond’s own, which carries its fixes. In the preview, a stack set up for Android for the first time gets OXT-Beyond’s.

It never updates itself

Help > Check for Updates asks GitHub for the latest release, shows its notes and opens its page. You install it; nothing downloads by itself.

AI help is off until you turn it on

The xTalk Assistant works with the model you choose, a local one included, and the xTalk MCP Bridge lets an agent build in the IDE. Neither runs until you set it up.

A dark appearance

Throughout the IDE, from the Tools palette to the Script Editor, following your system’s light or dark setting.

Where everything stands

Each feature with its status and the release it arrived in, so you know what to rely on today. Ready means the stable release, 0.2.7. Preview means the 0.2.8 developer previews: newer, and less tested.

Ready today

In the stable release, 0.2.7. Use it for your own work.

  • The desktop IDEWindows 10 or later (64-bit), macOS 11 or later (universal), and Linux on x86-64 and 64-bit ARM.
  • The IDE in a browser tabThe web IDE and the Stack Player, with nothing to install.
  • HTML5 standalonesTested automatically in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit.
  • AndroidTarget API 36 and App Bundles, as Google Play asks.
  • DatabasesrevDB with SQLite, MySQL, PostgreSQL and ODBC.
  • mergJSON and literal arraysJSONToArray and ArrayToJSON from mergJSON’s source, and literal arrays as in LiveCode 10.
  • HyperCard stacksImported with their scripts, pictures, colour and sound, and the scripts made to run.
  • The xTalk SuiteCryptography, BitTorrent, reliable UDP, WebRTC, Tor, Nostr, Bitcoin and Ethereum primitives and 2D physics, under the MIT licence.
  • AI helpThe xTalk Assistant, with any model, a local one included, and the xTalk MCP Bridge. Both are off until you turn them on.
  • Learning74 how-to lessons in Learn, the xTalk Academy course, and a dark appearance.

In the preview

  • Sound and game controllers dp.2.1audioxt mixes any number of sounds and records the microphone; gamepadxt reads Xbox, PlayStation and Switch pads.
  • Excel, WebP and barcodes dp.2.1xlsxt writes and reads .xlsx workbooks, webpxt reads and writes WebP pictures, and barcodext reads and makes QR codes and barcodes.

  • tsNet’s calls dp.2Answered by name by tsNetCompat, over libcurl.
  • MP4 on Windows dp.2Plays with no codec to install.
  • On your own server dp.1The web bundle puts the IDE and the Player on any web server, and the web host keeps your stacks there, behind a password.
  • WebSockets on the web dp.1In HTML5 standalones and the Stack Player.
  • Today’s database logins dp.1MySQL 8.4, and PostgreSQL’s SCRAM.

Experimental

There to try, and still taking shape.

  • Server pagesOXT-Beyond Server runs .lc pages on a web server.

Not available

Not in OXT-Beyond, and what to do instead.

  • iOSNo iOS build. A stack set up for iOS keeps its settings and builds for its other platforms.
  • Signed and notarized buildsThe Windows builds aren’t signed and the Mac app isn’t notarized: Windows may warn, and macOS asks you to allow it once. That matters to IT departments.
  • Commercial-only widgets and librariesA widget that isn’t installed opens as a placeholder and keeps its settings.
  • Password-protected stacksNot opened, as in LiveCode Community. Remove the password in the LiveCode that set it.
  • A closed licenceOXT-Beyond is GPL only. For a closed app shipped to customers, look at LiveCode’s commercial licence. In-house tools are another matter.

Not tested yet

Nobody has tried these yet. They may well work.

  • Stacks from LiveCode 10 and 11They may use syntax and widgets LiveCode 9 never had. The checker says whether their format is one OXT-Beyond reads.
  • Round trips back to LiveCodeLiveCode 8.1 and later read the 8.1 format OXT-Beyond saves, but going back hasn’t been tested. Keep a copy.
  • Your stackThat’s what the checker and the Stack Player are for.

Where it comes from

One road, carried on

The GPL is why this road exists: each stretch of it was free for the next to carry on.

  1. GPL

    LiveCode Community

    LiveCode Ltd’s GPLv3 edition of LiveCode. Its repositories have had no changes since July 2021, and are archived.

  2. 1.15

    OpenXTalk Lite

    A debranded LiveCode Community 9.6.3, begun by Terry Little in September 2023 and built by Tom Perry from 0.91 to 1.15, with the OpenXTalk community.

  3. 0.2.7

    OXT-Beyond

    The continuation of OpenXTalk Lite 1.15, over LiveCode Community’s 9.7 engine tree, built in the open on GitHub.

  4. next

    Whoever comes next

    The GPLv3 lets anyone carry it further, as OXT-Beyond carried on OpenXTalk Lite.

Thank you to LiveCode Ltd for releasing LiveCode Community under the GPL, and to Terry Little, Tom Perry and the OpenXTalk community for OpenXTalk Lite. The README’s “Where OXT-Beyond comes from” tells the whole story.

When a stack doesn’t open

Tell us, and it gets fixed

The project’s first rule makes a stack that stopped working a bug to fix, not a change to live with. Reports like that become fixes, and often tests.

  1. Copy the checker’s report

    Drop the stack on the checker and press Copy the report. It names the format, the widgets and the libraries, which is most of what anyone will ask.

  2. Say what happened

    What you did, what you expected and what you saw. An error’s text, word for word, helps most.

  3. Post it

    As an issue on GitHub, where a form asks for the rest, or on the OpenXTalk forum, where each release has its own thread. Attach a copy of the stack if you can share it.

Bring one stack along

Pick the stack you would miss most, check it, and play it in your browser. Then open a copy on your desktop, where the whole IDE is.
