In-house tools and the GPL

Tools for your own team cost nothing.

OXT-Beyond is free software under the GNU General Public License, version 3. The licence asks something of you only when you give someone else a copy. A tool that stays inside your organisation owes nothing: no fee, no royalty, and no source to publish.

The whole rule

It all turns on who gets a copy

The GPL calls giving someone a copy conveying. Until you convey, it asks nothing of you.

Using it

Never restricted

Installing OXT-Beyond, building in it and running what you build are yours to do, for any purpose, paid work included.

“This License explicitly affirms your unlimited permission to run the unmodified Program.”GPLv3, section 2

Keeping it inside

Nothing owed

Copies your organisation makes and uses for itself are not conveying. Put the tool on every desk you own, and keep its scripts private.

“You may make, run and propagate covered works that you do not convey, without conditions so long as your license otherwise remains in force.”GPLv3, section 2

Giving it to others

They get the source

A standalone carries the engine. Hand one to a customer, another company or the public, and its source goes with it, with the same GPL rights. You may still charge for it.

“You may charge any price or no price for each copy that you convey, and you may offer support or warranty protection for a fee.”GPLv3, section 4

Nothing owed

Your organisation: install it on every desk.

Copies an organisation makes and uses for itself are not conveying: it is making copies for itself. Put the standalone on every machine, or on a shared drive, and your scripts stay private.

“You may make, run and propagate covered works that you do not convey, without conditions so long as your license otherwise remains in force.”GPLv3, section 2

Nothing owed, by contract

A contractor: they can build it for you.

The GPLv3 lets others make or run the tool for you without that counting as giving it away, if they work only for you, under your direction and control, on terms that stop them copying your work outside that relationship. Put that in the contract. OXT-Beyond itself reaches them with its usual GPL rights; your own scripts stay yours.

“Those thus making or running the covered works for you must do so exclusively on your behalf, under your direction and control, on terms that prohibit them from making any copies of your copyrighted material outside their relationship with you.”GPLv3, section 2
GPLv3, section 2

Nothing owed

Your web server: serve it to anyone.

People who only use pages your server makes never receive a copy of the program, so nothing is conveyed, even when the public uses them. OXT-Beyond is under the GPLv3, not the AGPL, the licence written to ask for a network service’s source. OXT-Beyond Server, which runs .lc pages, is still experimental.

“Mere interaction with a user through a computer network, with no transfer of a copy, is not conveying.”GPLv3, section 0
GPLv3, section 0FSF FAQ: a modified program on a web site

Offer the source

The public, on the web: a web standalone is a copy.

A web standalone runs in the visitor’s browser: the page sends them the engine and your stack, and that is a copy. So publish the stack’s source beside it, under the GPL. On an intranet that only your own staff open, the same page stays in-house and owes nothing.

“To ‘convey’ a work means any kind of propagation that enables other parties to make or receive copies.”GPLv3, section 0
GPLv3, sections 0 and 6FSF FAQ: when source must be released

They get the source

Customers: selling it is fine. Closing it isn’t.

A standalone carries the engine, so whoever receives it must also be given its source, or a way to get it, and has the same GPL rights, passing it on included. You may still charge whatever you like for it. If the app has to stay closed, OXT-Beyond is the wrong tool: look at LiveCode’s commercial licence instead.

“You may charge any price or no price for each copy that you convey, and you may offer support or warranty protection for a fee.”GPLv3, section 4
FSF FAQ: selling copiesGPLv3, sections 4 to 6

Ask a lawyer

A related company: the grey area.

A subsidiary, a franchisee or a partner is a separate legal entity, so handing them a copy may count as conveying, even when it feels like one organisation. If it matters, ask a lawyer before you hand it over. Giving them the source with it, as you would a customer, is the safe course.

Honest about it

Where it doesn’t fit

People trust a project that says where it stops. Here is where this one does.

Not for these

  • Closed apps shipped to customers. OXT-Beyond comes only under the GPL, with no closed licence to buy. For a closed app, look at LiveCode’s commercial licence.
  • Apple’s App Store. Its terms clash with the GPL, and OXT-Beyond has no iOS build anyway.
  • Shipping it with code the GPL can’t sit beside. What you give to others must be under licences the GPL can carry. LiveCode Ltd’s written exception reaches only Microsoft’s ATL (revBrowser on Windows) and old OpenSSL, and not every part of OXT-Beyond carries it yet.

Good to know

  • Selling is allowed. Charge any price; the source and the same rights go with each copy. The FSF’s answer is “Yes, the GPL allows everyone to do this.”
  • Consultants: the client gets the source. A tool built for one client goes to them with its source and the GPL’s rights, which many clients like.
  • The xTalk Suite is MIT. The extensions built into OXT-Beyond, for cryptography, networking, physics and more, are under the MIT licence, which a GPL app can carry.
  • These rights stay. “All rights granted under this License are granted for the term of copyright on the Program, and are irrevocable provided the stated conditions are met.” (section 2)

Grey areas

Where to ask a lawyer

Two questions this page can’t settle for you, and one rule of thumb.

Is my stack itself GPL?

A stack is a file the engine reads and runs, so whether a stack you give away must be under the GPL too is debated. The usual view, as with LiveCode Community, is to treat it as GPL and give its source with it. For a stack that never leaves your organisation, the question doesn’t come up.

Subsidiaries, franchisees and partners

Each is a separate legal entity, so a copy given to one may count as conveying. If it matters, ask before you hand it over.

The rule of thumb

If a copy stays inside your organisation, nothing is owed. If it leaves, send its source with it, and you are doing what the GPL asks. For anything bigger, such as a commercial or closed-source plan, ask a lawyer who knows free-software licences.
