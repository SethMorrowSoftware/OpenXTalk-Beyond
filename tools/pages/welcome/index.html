Stacks, in the page

The Stack Player wears the website’s night sky. Open your own stack, or play the ones it comes with: a beat studio, a drum machine, games, a photo lab, a Kanban board.

  • Your files stay in your browser. A stack’s files go into the browser’s own storage for the site, not to a server.
  • It carries what a standalone would: the Data Grid and the other script libraries, revXML, revZip, revDB with SQLite, and the widgets made for the web.
  • It tells you plainly what works on the web, what works differently, and what does not.

Honest about it

What it isn’t, yet

Better to read it here than to find it out later.

Not available

  • iOS. OXT-Beyond has no iOS build. A stack set up for iOS keeps its settings and builds for its other platforms.
  • Signed and notarized builds. The Windows builds are not signed and the Mac app is not notarized, which matters to IT departments: Windows may warn, and macOS asks you to allow it once.
  • Commercial-only widgets and libraries. A widget that isn’t installed opens as a placeholder and keeps its settings.
  • Password-protected stacks. LiveCode Community never opened them either. Remove the password in the LiveCode that set it.
  • Closed apps for customers. OXT-Beyond is GPL only. For a closed app shipped to customers, look at LiveCode’s commercial licence.

Not tested yet

  • Stacks saved by LiveCode 10 and 11. They may use syntax and widgets that LiveCode 9 never had. LiveCode 11’s kinds of array, sequences and dictionaries, are in the preview, 0.2.8-dp.2.1.
  • Going back. OXT-Beyond saves in the 8.1 format that LiveCode 8.1 and later read, but round trips have not been tested. Keep a copy, and remember that Beyond-only syntax, such as literal arrays, won’t compile in LiveCode 9.
  • Your stack. That’s what the checker and the Stack Player are for. If something breaks, report it on GitHub or ask on the OpenXTalk forum: reports like that become fixes, and often tests.

Where every feature stands

New in the preview

3D, from plain script

box3dxt binds Box3D, Erin Catto’s 3D physics engine and the successor of Box2D, and render3dxt draws lit, textured 3D scenes into an ordinary image. Both are built into the preview, 0.2.8-dp.2.1, on Windows, macOS, Linux and Android, and in the web IDE and the Stack Player. Point at a clip, or tap it, to play.

Free for good

OXT-Beyond is GPLv3, and nobody, us included, can take that back. Every copy carries the same rights, and the whole project, with the workflows that build and test it, is on GitHub for anyone to fork and carry on. That is how OXT-Beyond itself began.

The downloads always offer the stable release and a preview of the next. And thank you to LiveCode Ltd: releasing LiveCode Community under the GPL is the reason OXT-Beyond, and OpenXTalk before it, can exist.
