- Sockets: closing a datagram listener (`accept datagram connections`) that had heard a sender no longer leaves an engine thread using a whole processor core on Linux and macOS, nor stops every other socket of the engine from being read: the names the listener gave its senders (`host:port`) kept its closed descriptor, which made the socket thread's select() fail at once, again and again. The names are still listed in the openSockets after their listener closes, as in LiveCode, until they are closed or their object is deleted; a write to one now fails with "error sending datagram" every time, where it went out through whatever had been given the closed descriptor's number since, and a listener opened again that hears the sender answers it through that name. At quit the engine frees its sockets under the lock the socket thread reads them with, which could corrupt the heap and abort or hang the engine as it quit (found by the xTalk Suite's OSC and Art-Net showcases) (#628)
