bitAnd
(operator)
Performs a "bitwise and" operation on the binary representation of two numbers.

Syntax:
{number1} bitAnd {number2}
----

Synonyms:

Params:
number1 ( ): The number1 and number2 are numbers, or expressions that evaluate to numbers, between zero and 4,294,967,295 (2^32 - 1).
number2 ( ): 

Examples:
0 bitAnd 1 -- evaluates to 0
----
3 bitAnd 2 -- in binary: 11 bitAnd 10; evaluates to 10; converted to 2
----

Description:
Use the bitAnd operator to operate directly on the bit|bits of two numbers.

To perform the bitAnd operation, OpenXTalk first converts both operand|operands to their binary equivalent, a string of ones and zeroes. 1 is equivalent to true, and 0 is equivalent to false.

For each bit of number1, OpenXTalk performs an and operation with the corresponding bit of number2 to produce a result. A bit is 1 if the corresponding bit|bits of number1 and number2 are both 1. Otherwise, the bit is 0.

Finally, the binary number thus created is converted back to decimal.
